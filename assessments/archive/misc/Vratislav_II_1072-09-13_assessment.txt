------------------------------

from 13 to 17, and the new additions are not Bohemians — they are German

Saxon territories well outside Bohemia's traditional sphere. Vratislav has
apparently prosecuted a successful campaign or series of diplomatic acquisitions

carries new complications.

The Jaromír problem was resolved. The rival bishop of Opava who ranked as the

to 41.7. In 1072 the Investiture Controversy is building to its crisis. Vratislav's
impiety penalty strongly suggests he is aligned with Heinrich against the papacy,
which is historically accurate. This is a structural condition rather than a
temporary problem.

Česlav Vrsovid has been heavily cultivated. His opinion sits at +125, entirely
from five rounds of sway scheming. In 1066 the Vrsovid count was flagged as a

I. THE REALM: HOLDINGS, VASSALS, AND INTERNAL BALANCE
------------------------------------------------------

The core Bohemian-Moravian vassals remain structurally similar. Konrád Přemyslid

Ota Přemyslid (Olomouc) holds steady at 864. Česlav Vrsovid (Žatec) is now

The new German vassals introduce a different risk profile. Ludwig Ludowinger

(Wolkenstein, 818 troops) has two low-control counties, the worst control profile
in the realm. These German counties represent a management burden. The claimant
faction around Otto is the most pressing internal concern in 1072.

The peasant faction (0 members currently) exists but is empty — a sign that
underlying tensions exist somewhere in the realm.


II. THE EXTERNAL SITUATION
--------------------------

The HRE is where the action is. Heinrich III Salian (5,143 levy, 162,100 power)
remains the formal overlord, now slightly stronger. The institutional relationship
is about to be tested: Investiture will pit the Emperor against the Pope, and
Bohemia — based on the Pope's opinion score — is squarely in Heinrich's camp.
Alignment has upside (reward with titles; historically Vratislav eventually
received a royal crown in 1085) but downside risk if Heinrich loses ground.

Ordulf Billung (Duke of Angria) is striking: power grew from 65,700 to 105,505,
making him one of the largest military forces in the local zone. Angria/Saxony
is historically the epicenter of Saxon resistance to Heinrich IV. A powerful
Billung duke adjacent to Bohemia's new German territories creates potential
friction — Meissen and Schmalkalden sit between Bohemia proper and the Saxon
sphere.

Kukovaitis II of Samogitia (Baltic pagan, 5,301 troops — up dramatically) is
now an outsized power to the north. Baltic pagan fervor has risen from 50 to

Poland (Bolesław III Piast, 3,535 troops) holds steady. Hungary (Salamon Árpád,
1,836 troops) has weakened considerably from 3,023 in 1066 — it may have lost

William of Normandie — now William of England — no longer appears as a nearby
force. His departure from central European politics is neutral for Bohemia.


III. THREATS AND ALLIES
-----------------------

Immediate concerns:
  1. Otto von Weimar (claimant faction) — the most urgent. A new vassal with a
     grievance and already organized. At 951 troops he can't threaten alone, but
     faction dynamics can attract others. Benno von Löningen (818 troops, two
     low-control counties) is a natural co-conspirator if discontent spreads.
     The German acquisitions need immediate diplomatic investment.

  2. Pope Anselmo's displeasure (-30) — not militarily threatening today, but
     the Investiture crisis will escalate. An excommunicated ruler suffers opinion
     penalties from all Catholic vassals. Managing church relationships through
     piety or a pilgrimage is worth considering as a hedge.

  3. Benno's low-control counties (2) — worst control situation in the realm.
     Reduces levy contribution and represents instability in newly acquired
     territory.

Medium-term concerns:
  4. Konrád of Brno (1,640 troops) — remains the overmighty internal vassal.
     No faction activity yet, but the structural concern is unchanged.

  5. The Billung-Saxon sphere — Ordulf Billung's power growth bears watching
     from the perspective of the new German counties. If the Saxon-Imperial
     conflict resumes, Meissen and Schmalkalden could become contested ground.

Allies and potential allies:
  - No formal alliances persist — the same gap identified in 1066. The most
    glaring diplomatic weakness remains unaddressed.
  - Heinrich Salian — the overlord relationship is the de facto alliance.
    Loyalty in the coming Investiture years could yield significant rewards.
  - Česlav Vrsovid (+125 opinion) — effectively turned into a client.
  - Hungary (weakened but Catholic) — still a natural southern partner. Its
    diminished state makes it more approachable.
  - Dedo II Wettin (Lausitz, 2,773 troops) — nearby Catholic HRE vassal who
    shares Bohemia's geopolitical frame. A formal alliance would stabilize
    the eastern flank and the new German acquisitions.

Bottom line:
  Vratislav has materially strengthened Bohemia — more troops, more territory,
  a resolved internal rival, a cultivated Vrsovid, and a trusted friend in
  Opava. The new risks are the product of success: German vassals who haven't
  been fully integrated, a deteriorated papal relationship, and no formal
  alliances to match the expanded realm. The Investiture Controversy is the
  defining external pressure — the right bet is probably to stay loyal to
  Heinrich while investing in piety as a hedge, and to focus internal energy
  on settling Otto von Weimar before the claimant faction finds more members.
