I. THE REALM: HOLDINGS, VASSALS, AND INTERNAL BALANCE OF POWER
---------------------------------------------------------------

Bohemia is a compact, well-developed duchy operating in peacetime with full
control across all counties. Vratislav's personal domain of seven holdings —
centered on Praha (development 12, the highest in the realm), with Boleslav,
Čáslav, Doudleby, Hradec, Litomeřice, and Plzeň all at development 8 — gives
him a solid economic base. The terrain is defensible: Praha and much of the
interior sits on hills, the northeastern flank (Hradec) rises to mountains,
and the Litomeřice valley is fertile farmland. The western border counties
(Děčín, Domažlice, Bílina) controlled by his barons are mountainous, providing
natural barriers.

Notably, Vratislav's direct domain consists entirely of city and church
holdings — his barons (republic-type vassals) and the bishop of Opava hold the
castles. This is an unusual position: he is commercially and religiously wealthy
but personally castle-light. His military strength (3,486 max, combat power
92,860) comes substantially from his men-at-arms cap (1,025) and personal
levies — a respectable force for a duke.

The vassal picture is more complicated. There are effectively three tiers:

Tier 1 — The Přemyslid cousins (Moravia):
  Konrád Přemyslid (Count of Brno, Moravia) is the dominant vassal by a wide
  margin: 1,509 troops, 36,640 combat power, holding three counties spanning
  forest, hills, and farmland. He commands nearly half as much force as
  Vratislav himself. Ota Přemyslid (Count of Olomouc) adds another 739 troops
  across two holdings. Together the Moravian Přemyslids control a contiguous
  territory to the southeast broadly comparable in raw military weight to
  Bohemia's western counties. They are family (Přemyslid house), which gives
  Vratislav house-head leverage, but also means they have dynastic standing to
  push back.

Tier 2 — The problematic vassals:
  Jaromír Přemyslid (Count of Opava, bishop — theocracy vassal) is Vratislav's
  declared mutual rival and holds the northeastern salient toward Silesia with
  627 troops. As a theocracy vassal he cannot be easily dispossessed, and as a
  rival he is an active source of negative opinion. He sits on a forested border
  county — strategically exposed but also relatively isolated.

  Česlav Vrsovid (Count of Žatec) is the sole non-Přemyslid feudal count,
  holding Žatec in the fertile northwest on farmlands. The Vrsovids historically
  have been rivals of the Přemyslids; his 573-troop force is modest but his
  political independence is a long-term concern.

Tier 3 — The minor barons:
  Seven city-baron vassals (republic type) contribute minimal levy (~70 each)
  but represent the urban commercial base of Bohemia. They are politically
  negligible individually.

The net assessment is that Bohemia is internally stable but carrying a
structural tension: Konrád in Moravia is powerful enough that any future
dispute would be serious, and the Jaromír rivalry is a live irritant.
Vratislav's house-head hooks on his three sons (Boleslav, Bořivoj, Vladislav)
are useful succession insurance but do nothing for the vassal problem today.

------------------------------------------------------

  crushingly dominant. Bohemia with 92,860 combat power is one of the HRE's more
  militarily capable duchies, giving Vratislav leverage while also making him a
  desirable campaign target for Heinrich. The year 1066 is precisely the
  beginning of the era in which Heinrich will face the great Saxon revolts and

Local neighbors (eastern Europe, immediate zone):

  Pomerania. Currently no direct threat, but no alliance either.

  Siemomysl of Pomerania (2,772 troops, slavic pagan), Kasper Lutici of Luticia

  Dedo Wettin (Duke of Lausitz, 2,057 troops) and Otto von Weimar (Duke of
  Meissen, 1,762 troops) are HRE vassals in neighboring duchies to the west

  William de Normandie (7,034 troops, 193,100 power) is at this date five days

III. THREATS AND ALLIES
-----------------------

Immediate threats (internal):
  1. Jaromír Přemyslid (rival, Count of Opava) — the most urgent destabilizing
     relationship inside the realm. A sitting rival with 627 troops in a border
     county is an opinion drain and a potential factional anchor. Worth
     neutralizing diplomatically or through succession if possible.

  2. Konrád of Brno (overmighty vassal) — not currently hostile, but his
     levy-to-ruler ratio (~43% of Vratislav's own) is in the range where faction
     leadership becomes plausible as the game progresses. Watch for faction
     formation.

Medium-term external threats:
  3. Poland (Bolesław Piast) — the only local secular ruler with a substantial
     army outside the HRE. If Poland expands toward Silesia or pressures the
     Přemyslid sphere, Bohemia would feel it.

  4. HRE demands — Heinrich's coming crises may pull Bohemia into imperial wars
     or create opportunities/instabilities depending on which side Vratislav
     supports.

  5. Pagan northern neighbors — low near-term threat individually, but
     Pomerania + Luticia + Veletia together exceed Bohemia's total strength.
     Northern border remains exposed.

Potential allies:
  1. Hungary — closest Catholic neighbor to the south, shared faith, no current
     conflict. A marriage alliance with the Árpáds would be the most natural
     diplomatic move.

  2. HRE co-vassals (Wettin, Weimar) — share Heinrich as overlord and face
     similar pressures from pagan neighbors. Lateral alliances within the
     imperial structure cost relatively little and provide mutual defense.

  3. The papacy — Catholic faith with armed pilgrimages and crusade tenets means
     the Pope is an indirect tool. Strong Church relations help with Jaromír's
     bishop problem and position Bohemia favorably during Investiture debates.

  4. Internal Přemyslid consolidation — Konrád and Ota are Přemyslids. Treating
     them well, arranging cross-marriages, and maintaining dynasty unity could
     convert the overmighty-vassal risk into a strength, especially if Poland or
     a pagan neighbor threatened Moravia.

Bottom line:
  Vratislav is in a position of quiet strength — no wars, full control,
  competitive combat power, protected by HRE membership. The vulnerabilities are
  diplomatic (zero alliances, one internal rival) rather than immediate military.
  Priority moves: neutralize or accommodate Jaromír, secure a marriage alliance
  southward toward Hungary or laterally toward a fellow HRE duke, and watch
  Konrád of Brno carefully as the dominant vassal.
