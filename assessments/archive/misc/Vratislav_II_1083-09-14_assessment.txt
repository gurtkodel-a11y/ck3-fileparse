WHAT HAS CHANGED: 1072 → 1083
------------------------------

The headline is the title. Vratislav is no longer Duke of Bohemia — he is
King of Bohemia. This elevation, which the 1072 assessment identified as the
likely reward for loyalty to Heinrich during the Investiture Controversy, has
been delivered. The royal title changes Vratislav's position in the HRE's
pecking order, his leverage over vassals, and the scope of future ambition.
Everything else in this assessment should be read against that backdrop.

The realm has grown again. Vassal count expanded from 17 to 24, and the new
additions are uniformly German: Wettins (Osterland, Merseburg, Wittenberg),
Ludowingers (Orlamünde, though Schmalkalden is now a separate Ludowinger
branch under Berengar), von Dagsburg (Görlitz), von Baerenburg (Sommerfeld),
Renart (Spreewald), and a new theocracy vassal Viktor von Arnstein (Naumburg).
The German integration project that began with four acquisitions in 1072 now
extends deep into eastern Thuringia and Saxony.

The critical diplomatic achievement of the period is the Konrád alliance.
Konrád Přemyslid — flagged in 1066 as the structural overmighty-vassal risk,
still dominant in 1072 — is now a negotiated ally with +55 opinion. This is the
single most important internal stabilization of the whole eleven-year arc.

Development has finally moved. Praha reached 14 (from 12 in both 1066 and 1072);
the six secondary holdings all advanced to 10 (from 8). Domain is no longer
stagnating.

Domain levy capacity, however, dropped sharply: from 3,524 in 1072 to 2,471
now. Total max fell from 4,650 to 3,707 despite more vassals. The most likely
explanations are domain reorganization when the royal title was granted (perhaps
Vratislav was required to hold fewer counties directly), or that some holdings
were regranted to new vassals. Whatever the cause, his personal military reach
has shrunk relative to the expanded realm — a meaningful shift.

The papal relationship, which stood at -30 in 1072 (Investiture alignment),
no longer appears as a notable opinion modifier. Either the immediate crisis
has eased or the penalty decayed. Heinrich's opinion sits at +30 (a generous
gift at a feast), suggesting the Imperial relationship remains warm.

On the dark side: Jan, the trusted friend installed as Count of Opava in 1072
to replace the hostile Jaromír, has attempted to murder someone close to
Vratislav. Vratislav holds -70 opinion of Jan for this ("attempted_murder_
close_family_crime"). This is a serious betrayal — but Jan holds a theocracy
vassalage that cannot be revoked by normal means. The problem created by
Jaromír (an unremovable hostile bishop) has been recreated in new form.


I. THE REALM: HOLDINGS, VASSALS, AND INTERNAL BALANCE
------------------------------------------------------

Vratislav's personal domain is the familiar seven holdings — Praha, Boleslav,
Čáslav, Doudleby, Hradec, Litomeřice, Plzeň — all at full control. Praha at
development 14 is a respectable capital; the remaining six at 10 are solidly
average. Terrain profile is defensible: hills and mountains dominate the core
(Praha, Čáslav, Doudleby on hills; Hradec in mountains), farmlands in
Litomeřice, plains in Boleslav and Plzeň. Commercially and ecclesiastically
wealthy, still castle-light.

The vassal roster at 24 divides into four distinct clusters:

Cluster 1 — The Přemyslid-Bohemian core:
  Konrád Přemyslid (Brno) remains the dominant vassal at 1,916 total troops —
  still the largest individual levy by a significant margin. But the political
  relationship has transformed: he is now a negotiated ally (+55 opinion,
  courtly title creation +30, sway schemes x2). The 1066-era "overmighty
  vassal" risk has been converted into a structural anchor for the realm.
  Ota Přemyslid (Olomouc, 1,014 troops) and Jan (Opava, 1,129 troops) fill
  out the Moravian-northeastern flank. Jan's situation is covered below.

Cluster 2 — The cultivated Bohemian barons:
  Česlav Vrsovid (Žatec, +125 opinion) and the city-baron republicans are
  effectively loyal clients. No faction flags among them. The western border
  strip — Děčín (Hroznata), Domažlice (Božepor), Bílina (Záviš) — remains
  mountain-terrain barons in the castle-holder mold. Neuša (Beroun) and
  Beneš (Vyšehrad) round out the Bohemian baronage.

Cluster 3 — The established German vassals:
  Ludwig II Ludowinger (Orlamünde, 1,112 troops) and Hinrik von Löningen
  (Wolkenstein, 1,059 troops) are the strongest non-Přemyslid German counts.
  Hinrik is in a liberty faction — the Löningen family has now been in some
  form of faction across two saves, shifting from implied discontent in 1072
  (low-control counties) to formal rebellion demand in 1083. Otto von Weimar
  (Meissen, 945 troops) remains stubbornly in a claimant faction — eleven
  years on, this grievance has never been resolved. The two faction members
  individually lack the strength to act, but together they represent 2,004
  troops in organized opposition within the German territories.

Cluster 4 — The newly absorbed Germans:
  Konrad II Wettin (Osterland, 1,257 troops, 3 holdings) is the most
  significant new arrival — larger than most earlier German additions. Dedo III
  Wettin (Merseburg, 926 troops), Friedrich von Dagsburg (Görlitz, 865 troops),
  Dietrich III Wettin (Wittenberg, 512 troops, zero levy — likely recently
  acquired or in some disrupted state), and the remaining minor counts
  (Spreewald, Schmalkalden, Sommerfeld) fill out the eastern German fringe.
  Viktor von Arnstein holds Naumburg as a theocracy vassal with a warm +60
  opinion. Ludwig Renart has -14 from "opinion_of_predecessor" — a holdover
  grievance from whoever Vratislav succeeded or displaced in that comital seat.

The Jan (Opava) problem warrants separate attention. Jan was Vratislav's
personal friend, placed in Opava in 1072 as a trusted replacement for the
hostile Jaromír. By 1083 Vratislav holds -70 opinion of Jan for attempted
murder against his close family. Jan holds +15 (grateful opinion) — presumably
from the original appointment. A theocracy vassal cannot be revoked. The
northeast is now held by a man Vratislav regards as a would-be murderer of
his kin, in a position he cannot be removed from. Jaromír (now a mutual
nemesis, still unlanded in a foreign court) is a personal antagonist; Jan
is a structural governance crisis waiting to deepen.


II. THE EXTERNAL SITUATION
--------------------------

The royal title elevates Bohemia's formal standing within the HRE. Vratislav
is no longer simply "Duke of Bohemia subject to Heinrich" — he is a king within
the empire, nominally equal to the kings of Poland and Hungary in rank, even
if subordinate to the Emperor in feudal fact. This affects how neighboring
rulers perceive the realm and what diplomatic moves are available.

Heinrich III Salian remains the formal overlord at 7,329 troops and 198,148
combat power — larger than in 1072. The relationship is warm (+30). But the
Investiture Crisis' arc is still running; Bohemia's ongoing loyalty yields
rewards but keeps the papal complication potentially active.

Poland (Bolesław III Piast, 4,468 troops) has grown substantially from 3,535
in 1072 — Poland is expanding, most likely eastward into Ruthenia or against
the pagan belt. This is the most significant change in Bohemia's immediate
external environment. A strengthening Poland on the northeast, combined with
the Bohemian realm now extending deep into Thuringia and Saxony to the west,
means Vratislav is developing a realm that stretches across two very different
strategic frontiers simultaneously.

The pagan northern belt persists. Jaromil Lutici (Duke of Luticia, 2,592
troops, slavic pagan), Swietibor of Pomerania (2,195 troops), and Krutoj
Wizlawid of Veletia (2,109 troops) remain unconverted and collectively large.
These powers have not consolidated but they have not shrunk either. Bohemia's
German expansion has brought the realm closer to their territory.

Hungary: Géza (Duke of Nyitra, 3,097 troops) has grown considerably and holds
a much stronger position than the weakened Salamon Árpád of the 1072 assessment.
László II Árpád of Transylvania (2,446 troops) is a personal friend of
Vratislav's — a valuable relationship on the southeastern frontier.

Magnus Billung (Duke of Angria, 4,296 troops) is the largest military power
in the immediate German neighborhood. The Billung-Saxon sphere continues to
loom over Bohemia's western acquisitions. If Saxon resistance to Heinrich were
to resume seriously, the German counties would be in the crossfire.

The Byzantine Empire (Isaakios II Komnenos, 5,869 troops) is a significant
nearby power at the "nearby" zone — relevant insofar as it anchors the Orthodox
bloc in the south and east. Matilda of Tuscany (4,130 troops) remains the
Italian counterweight to Imperial power. The broader European landscape is
stable for Bohemia but crowded.


III. THREATS AND ALLIES
-----------------------

Immediate concerns:
  1. Otto von Weimar (claimant faction, 945 troops) — PERSISTENT. This grievance
     has now survived two full assessment cycles without resolution. At 945 troops
     Otto alone cannot threaten the realm, and his faction has not grown (still
     one member), but the longer an organized claimant faction exists, the more
     opportunity it has to attract sympathizers. Eleven years of inaction suggests
     this is a hard problem — possibly Otto holds a claim that cannot be easily
     bought out, or there is a rights dispute with no clean resolution. Active
     resolution must be a priority before the faction grows.

  2. Hinrik von Löningen (liberty faction, 1,059 troops) — NEW. The Löningen
     holding passed to a new holder since 1072 but the political culture of the
     county remained fractious. A liberty faction aims to reduce Vratislav's
     control over the vassal — if successful it would undermine his ability to
     govern the German territories. Combined with Otto's claimant faction, there
     are now two organized factions among the German vassals totaling ~2,000 troops.

  3. Jan of Opava (theocracy, -70 from Vratislav, +15 from Jan) — STRUCTURAL
     CRISIS. An unremovable vassal who attempted murder against the king's family
     sits in the northeastern salient controlling 1,129 troops. Options are
     limited: theocracy vassals cannot be revoked. Assassination schemes are risky
     and the character is presumably on guard. The Opava problem is now worse than
     the original Jaromír situation because the relationship failure was personal
     rather than purely political.

  4. Konrad II Wettin (Osterland, 1,257 troops) — WATCH. As the strongest new
     German vassal, Wettin could become the next "Konrád of Brno" problem in the
     German territories if his opinion turns. No faction flag yet; worth monitoring.

Medium-term concerns:
  5. Poland (Bolesław III Piast, 4,468 troops, growing) — the northeastern
     external power is strengthening. As long as Poland expands eastward into the
     pagan and Rus territories rather than westward toward Bohemia this is benign,
     but the trajectory should be watched.

  6. The German realm-management problem generally — 24 vassals means a larger
     opinion management surface area. Ludwig Renart's predecessor-grievance and
     Adelheid's chancellor-induced displeasure are minor individually, but the
     German counties as a bloc are not well-integrated. A wave of faction
     membership among the German counts would be a serious crisis.

Allies and assets:
  1. Konrád Přemyslid (negotiated ally, +55 opinion) — the resolution of the
     1066-era overmighty vassal problem is the most important diplomatic success
     of the period. A loyal Konrád anchors Moravia and backstops any internal
     challenge.

  2. László II Árpád (Duke of Transylvania, personal friend) — a Hungarian duke
     as a personal friend provides informal alignment with Hungary without a
     formal treaty. The southeastern frontier is quiet.

  3. Česlav Vrsovid (+125 opinion) — still a client, unchanged.

  4. Viktor von Arnstein (+60 opinion, theocracy Naumburg) — a friendly bishop
     in the German territories is an asset against the faction-prone lay counts.

  5. Heinrich III Salian (+30 opinion) — the Imperial relationship remains warm.
     The royal crown itself is the most tangible product of this alignment.

  6. Sons: Vladislav is a mutual friend and a knight in court; Boleslav, Bořivoj,
     and Přibyslav are present. Succession is in better shape than it appears —
     the house-head hooks on eleven Přemyslid family members give Vratislav
     leverage over dynastic behavior.

Bottom line:
  Vratislav II is now a king, and Bohemia is now a kingdom. The arc from 1066
  is remarkable: from a compact duchy with one rival bishop, no alliances, and
  an overmighty cousin, to a multi-ethnic realm spanning Bohemia, Moravia, and
  eastern Germany, with a royal title, a loyal Konrád, a cultivated Vrsovid,
  and a warm relationship with the Emperor.

  The vulnerabilities are the product of this scale. Two organized factions in
  the German territories, an irremovable vassal who committed a crime against the
  king's family, and a growing Poland on the eastern flank are serious problems
  that did not exist in 1066. The realm has grown faster than its institutions.

  The highest-priority internal actions: resolve or contain the Otto von Weimar
  claimant faction before it attracts the Löningen liberty faction and others;
  find a path to neutralize Jan of Opava (this may require patience — waiting
  for his death and choosing a replacement carefully). The highest-priority
  external action: formalize the relationship with László of Transylvania into
  a proper alliance, closing the southeastern flank. With Konrád secured and
  the Emperor warm, the core is stable — the German periphery is where Bohemia's
  next decade will be decided.
