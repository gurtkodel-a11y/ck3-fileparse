WHAT HAS CHANGED: 1083 → 1088
------------------------------

The realm has been reorganized. The most striking number in this save is the
vassal count: 24 in 1083, 15 in 1088. This is not a contraction — it is a
deliberate consolidation. Vratislav resolved his German management problem not
by expelling vassals but by restructuring them under two new duchy-level
intermediaries.

Konrád Přemyslid is now Duke of Moravia. He holds the duchy title Vratislav
created for him, and his opinion stands at +225 — the product of three courtly
title creations, two duchy grants, two sway schemes, and a friendly alliance
forged over seventeen years. What began in 1066 as the realm's most dangerous
structural risk (an overmighty cousin commanding nearly half Vratislav's levy)
has become its most valuable political asset. Ota Přemyslid and Jan of Opava
both dropped from Vratislav's direct vassal list — they are now sub-vassals of
Moravia under Konrád, which explains why Jan's -70 crime opinion persists in
Vratislav's records even though he no longer appears in the contracts list.

Konrad II Wettin was elevated to Duke of Lausitz with similar mechanics: a
duchy title grant, a declared war at some point (probably the method of
acquisition), and subsequent sway investment producing +60 net opinion. The
sprawling Thuringian and Saxon counties that were individually unmanageable in
1083 are now organized under Wettin's duchy. Otto von Weimar, Hinrik von
Löningen, Friedrich von Dagsburg, and the remaining minor German counts have
disappeared from Vratislav's direct vassal list — absorbed into Lausitz as
sub-vassals or otherwise removed from play.

Both factions are gone. The eleven-year-old Otto von Weimar claimant faction
and the Hinrik von Löningen liberty faction that appeared in 1083 no longer
exist as organized challenges. Hinrik holds -30 (insulted) opinion — Vratislav
apparently chose the confrontational path to resolve the Löningen problem —
but he is no longer Vratislav's direct vassal.

Military strength jumped substantially: total max from 3,707 to 5,057, levy
from 2,471 to 3,819, combat power from 95,732 to 130,640. This is the sharpest
single-period growth in personal military capacity of any interval in the arc.
Domain development also continued its post-1072 advance: Praha reached 15,
all secondaries at 10–11, total development 80 (up from 74).


I. THE REALM: HOLDINGS, VASSALS, AND INTERNAL BALANCE
------------------------------------------------------

Vratislav's personal domain is the stable seven-holding core of Bohemia —
Praha (15), Boleslav (11), Doudleby (11), Hradec (11), Litomeřice (11),
Plzeň (11), Čáslav (10). Full control across all counties. Building progress
continues steadily: agriculture and resource categories expanded since 1083.
This domain generates the foundation for Vratislav's record personal levy of
3,819.

The vassal structure now has two tiers instead of three:

Tier 1 — The Duchy-level pillars:
  Konrád Přemyslid, Duke of Moravia: 2,506 total troops, 3 holdings, forest/
  hills/farmlands terrain. Opinion +225. Negotiated ally. Sub-vassals include
  Ota Přemyslid (Olomouc) and Jan of Opava. Konrád is now more than an
  overmighty vassal who was neutralized — he functions as a co-administrator
  of the eastern realm. His military contribution to any conflict Vratislav
  faces would be enormous, and his loyalty has been constructed deliberately
  over two decades.

  Konrad II Wettin, Duke of Lausitz: 1,898 total troops, 3 holdings, plains
  terrain. Opinion +60 (received duchy title, sway schemes, offset by a
  declared-war penalty). The German territories are now his administrative
  responsibility. He is not as personally cultivated as Konrád, and the war
  event that preceded his duchy elevation suggests the relationship required
  coercion before stabilizing.

Tier 2 — The direct count and baron layer:
  The remaining direct vassals are almost entirely Bohemian. The Ludowinger
  counts (Ludwig II of Orlamünde, Berengar of Schmalkalden) are the only
  remaining German feudal counts with direct contracts to Vratislav. Česlav
  Vrsovid (Žatec, +125 opinion) holds the fertile northwest. The Bohemian
  baronage — Neuša, Záviš, Beneš, the city republicans — is stable and
  largely low-leverage.

  Dedo III Wettin (Count of Merseburg, 773 troops) is technically a direct
  vassal with one low-control county. He likely should sit under Konrad
  Wettin's Lausitz duchy but for some reason retains a direct contract.
  He holds -10 "angry opinion" — a mild concern worth monitoring.

Notable gaps and structural questions:
  Jan of Opava remains a personal grievance (-70 from attempted murder) even
  after disappearing from direct vassals. As Konrád's sub-vassal, Jan is now
  Konrád's management problem — but if Konrád's loyalty were ever to shift,
  Jan's unresolved crime would be a live destabilizing element. The consolidation
  under Moravia resolved the governance headache while deferring the moral and
  political taint.

  The Ota Přemyslid situation: Ota (Count of Olomouc) now sits under Konrád
  as a sub-vassal. Two Přemyslid counts under a Přemyslid duke creates a
  compact Přemyslid territorial bloc in Moravia. This is fine while Konrád
  is loyal — it becomes a coherent rival if that changes.


II. THE EXTERNAL SITUATION
--------------------------

The most important external change is Hungary. Géza, formerly Duke of Nyitra
(3,097 troops in 1083), is now King of Hungary with 4,573 troops. Hungary
has grown from a southeastern regional power into a significant Catholic
kingdom. László II Árpád (Duke of Transylvania, personal friend of Vratislav)
is presumably now a Hungarian royal vassal. This consolidates the southeastern
flank but changes the nature of the relationship: rather than a friendly peer
duke, Vratislav's friend László operates under a strengthened overlord.

Poland (Bolesław III Piast, 5,047 troops) continues to grow and now roughly
matches Hungary in military weight. Poland sits immediately on Bohemia's
northeastern border. Both kingdoms are now in the 4,000–5,000 troop range —
comparable to each other, significantly stronger than they were in 1066.

The pagan northern belt is unchanged in character: Jaromil Lutici (Luticia,
2,843 troops), Swietibor of Pomerania (2,535), Krutoj Wizlawid of Veletia
(2,225). These three slavic pagan powers remain unconverted and collectively
formidable. Bohemia's extended German territories (Lausitz, Schmalkalden)
now border this belt more directly.

Magnus Billung (Duke of Angria, 4,356 troops) remains the largest military
presence in the immediate German neighborhood. The Saxon sphere continues to
overshadow the German counties.

Heinrich III Salian's HRE holds at 7,979 troops and 196,699 power — slightly
grown from 1083. The formal overlord relationship remains warm (no negative
Imperial opinion appears in the report). The Investiture Crisis' acute phase
appears to have passed for Bohemia — the -30 papal opinion of 1072 and the
factional disruptions it implied have fully dissipated.

Matilda of Tuscany (4,640 troops) has grown and remains the papacy's principal
secular defender. Her position is relevant insofar as she anchors the
pro-papal faction against Heinrich — a dynamic Vratislav navigated carefully
in earlier decades.


III. THREATS AND ALLIES
-----------------------

Immediate concerns:
  1. Konrád Přemyslid's structural weight — this remains the fundamental
     long-term question of the Bohemian realm. Konrád as Duke of Moravia
     now commands 2,506 troops, controls three counties, and sits atop
     two Přemyslid sub-vassals. His opinion is +225 and the alliance is
     intact, but no ruler is immortal and no opinion score is permanent.
     Succession — both Vratislav's and Konrád's — is the variable that
     could transform this asset back into a threat. The current architecture
     requires maintaining the Přemyslid consensus across a generational
     transition.

  2. Konrad Wettin's incomplete integration — the Lausitz duchy is a more
     recent and more coercive creation than Moravia. The war event, the
     "declared_war -25" opinion penalty still visible, and Dedo III Wettin's
     -10 "angry opinion" suggest the German consolidation remains fresher
     and less settled. Dedo's status as a direct vassal when he would logically
     belong under Wettin may indicate unresolved jurisdictional tension.

  3. Hinrik von Löningen (-30 insulted, no longer direct vassal) — the
     insult approach to resolving the liberty faction seems to have worked
     structurally (he's no longer a direct threat) but created a personal
     enemy. If Hinrik resurfaces with claims or faction membership under
     a Lausitz sub-structure, the problem could recur.

  4. Jan of Opava (-70, now sub-vassal under Konrád) — deferred rather than
     resolved. Vratislav cannot touch him, and now only Konrád can. The
     problem migrated up the vassal chain.

  5. Poland (5,047 troops, growing) — the most significant external threat
     trajectory. Poland is now at rough military parity with Hungary and
     significantly larger than any single internal actor. If Poland turns
     aggressive toward Bohemia's Silesian or Moravian frontier, the response
     would need to be a coalition. The Konrád-Moravia alliance would be the
     cornerstone of any such defense.

Allies and assets:
  1. Konrád Přemyslid, Duke of Moravia (+225, negotiated ally) — the crown
     jewel of twenty-two years of relationship investment. Bohemia's most
     important military and political asset, bar the Imperial connection.

  2. Heinrich III Salian (warm relationship, no visible negatives) — the
     HRE umbrella provides strategic depth against any external threat that
     would trigger imperial concern. Poland attacking Bohemia is Poland
     attacking an HRE member.

  3. László II Árpád (Duke of Transylvania, mutual friend) — warm personal
     relationship with a Hungarian duke, now operating under a stronger
     Hungarian king. The value of this connection depends on whether Géza's
     Hungary is well-disposed toward Bohemia.

  4. Česlav Vrsovid (+125, Count of Žatec) — still a completely cultivated
     client in the western Bohemian heartland. Stable.

  5. Favor hooks on Ermessinde and Peter (expires 1094) — active leverage
     on two individuals for some specific purpose not visible in this report.
     Their -20 "demanded hook" opinions suggest the hooks were recently
     called in and the transaction is still fresh.

  6. Sons: Vladislav (mutual friend, knight), Boleslav, Bořivoj (knights),
     Přibyslav, Kuna in court. Succession pool is healthy, and house-head
     hooks over seventeen Přemyslid family members give structural control
     over dynastic behavior.

Bottom line:
  The 1083–1088 period completed a fundamental restructuring of the realm.
  Vratislav solved his German management problem by creating intermediate
  duchy administrators, and he solved his Konrád problem by transforming it
  into a partnership. The result: zero active factions, fifteen manageable
  direct vassals instead of twenty-four, the strongest personal military
  capacity of the whole arc, and a Přemyslid cousin bound to the realm by
  two decades of carefully accumulated gratitude.

  The risks are now concentrated at the strategic level rather than the
  governance level. The Wettin duchy needs time to settle. Poland is growing.
  Succession — both Vratislav's succession and Konrád's — is the horizon
  problem that the current architecture has not yet been tested against. And
  Jan of Opava remains unresolved, an ember in Konrád's house.

  For the near term, however, Bohemia is at a peak of internal order not
  seen since the 1066 starting point — but now atop a much larger and better-
  developed realm. The twenty-two-year arc from nervous duke to settled king
  has been, by any measure, a success.
