IX. NARRATIVE THREADS THIS OPENS
---------------------------------

The Heir's Virtue: Břetislav is compassionate, honest, generous, and charming —
all at once, in a world that rarely rewards that combination. If he survives to
inherit a duchy, he will be a different kind of Přemyslid lord than his father.
Whether that is a triumph or a vulnerability depends on what Bohemia faces when
he takes power.

The Stepchildren: Judita, Břetislav, and Ludmila lost their mother at ages 6, 4,
and 2. They have grown up under Šwiétosława, who is deceitful and has her own
three sons in the succession pool. The game does not simulate maternal warmth or
its absence, but the character math is suggestive. Whether Šwiétosława has been
a fair steward of the elder children's interests is a question the player can
interpret as they like.

The Brothers' Problem: Boleslav (diligent), Bořivoj (greedy+trusting), and
Vladislav (trusting+ill) are three very different younger sons from the same
mother. They will grow up in Břetislav's shadow. Bolt of ambition in the wrong
direction is the main risk — especially from Bořivoj. The hooks Vratislav holds
are the right instrument for managing this, but they will need to be deployed
thoughtfully as the boys mature.

The Alliance Calendar: Judita is 10, and she becomes a marriage asset in ~1072.
That is early in Vratislav's reign — he will likely still be a duke, not yet the
king he becomes in the 1083 playtest. The right marriage for her probably targets
a neighbor whose goodwill is worth purchasing now: a German count on the western
border, a Polish lord who reinforces the Šwiétosława connection, or a Bohemian
vassal whose loyalty benefits from a dynastic tie.
