II. SHORT-TERM (~1071–1077)
================================================================

[FOREIGN] Form the Hungary alliance (overdue)
  Context: No alliances exist. This was the first-priority short-term foreign
    plan from 1066, and it was not executed. Hungary (Salamon Árpád, 3,166
    troops, Catholic) remains the clearest alliance target.
  Why still valid: A formal Hungarian alliance is the most natural external
    partner given faith, adjacency, and no competing interests. The Polish
    connection via Šwiétosława is a potential second lever but is less
    tractable (Bolesław III Piast succession may have changed the relationship).
  Mechanism: Judita's marriage could be directed at a Hungarian-adjacent target
    if no Styrian/Austrian option materializes. Otherwise, seek a standard
    alliance via council diplomacy or marriage between secondary lines.

[FAMILY] Assess the Oda-Břetislav situation by ~1073
  Who: Oda (id 37772, age 15, Franconian, daughter of Otto von Weimar)
  Context: Oda and Břetislav share a mutual crush. She's greedy, patient,
    sadistic, charming with effective Prowess 10 and Intrigue 7. Her father
    Otto holds -25 declared_war opinion of Vratislav.
  Decision to make by ~1073: Is this a marriage to encourage or redirect?
    PRO: Ties the von Weimar line more closely to Bohemia; could reduce Otto's
    residual hostility through a family bond; the crush is mutual (lower
    resistance than an arranged match).
    CON: Sadistic trait is a concern for the heir's spouse and the next
    generation. Franconian culture adds integration friction. The connection
    is to a recently conquered vassal, which sends a particular signal about
    Bohemia's relationship to its new Saxon subjects.
  If redirecting: Břetislav will need an alternative match identified. His
    profile (age 13 in 1071, heir, effective Dipl 15) will attract interest
    from larger dynasties. A Hungarian or Polish match is geopolitically
    stronger; a Saxon match sends a consolidation signal.

[COURT] Grant Duchy of Meissen to Reginar (decided)
  Decision: Grant d_meissen to Reginar von Dresden (Count of Dresden).
  Rationale: Reginar is the most tractable of the Saxon bloc — content + trusting,
    the disposition least likely to weaponize a duchy against us. The von Weimar
    line (-25 opinion) is ineligible for obvious reasons. Ludwig (Ludowinger) and
    Eberhard have aging +20 modifiers that expire ~1081; relying on temporary
    goodwill for a permanent grant is the wrong foundation. Benno von Lóningen
    is a vassal-knight with an unknown personal profile.
  Execution note: The grant makes Reginar a duke-tier vassal. His former peers
    (Ludwig, Eberhard, Benno, Otto — all county-tier) become his de jure subjects
    where their counties fall inside d_meissen. This is the point: placing a
    content+trusting character atop a potentially restive bloc is stabilizing.
  Possible follow-up: Renegotiate Reginar's vassal contract after the grant —
    reducing his levy contribution (if feasible under Feudal contract terms) gives
    him more standing troops relative to his new subjects, improving his ability
    to actually hold the duchy. Worth checking whether levy renegotiation is
    available and at what gold/prestige cost.

[FOREIGN] Forge marriage alliances with Saxon vassal households (decided)
  Decision: Pursue marriages between Přemyslid court members and children of
    the new Saxon vassal households. Target families: Ludwig Ludowinger (children
    include Uta, Adalheid, Berengar — roughly Judita's generation), Benno von
    Lóningen (children Geilana, Liutswind, Adalgar), Reginar's son Liudolf.
  Rationale: Marriage bonds don't change opinions directly, but they create
    inter-household dependencies that make rebellion more costly to the noble
    family as a whole. Marriages into the von Weimar line (Otto) are lower
    priority given the -25 declared_war wound, but Oda↔Břetislav is already
    being evaluated separately.
  Timing note: Ludwig's +20 goodwill expires ~1081. Approaching his household
    while the modifier is active is more likely to succeed. If negotiations are
    started within 1–2 years, the favorable context exists.
  Children available from our side: Ludmila (age 11, opens ~1074), Boleslav
    (age 8, opens ~1079), Bořivoj (age 7, opens ~1080). Boleslav and Bořivoj
    may be too young for the immediate window; Ludmila is the most relevant
    piece. See [FAMILY] Ludmila's marriage planning above for the tension between
    external alliance and internal (Vrsovid) match.

III. LONG-TERM (~1077–1090)
================================================================

[DYNASTIC] Břetislav's succession readiness
  Target state when Vratislav dies (whenever that is):
    — Diplomacy education tier 3–4 (on track given current trajectory)
    — Clear heir status, no serious contention from half-brothers
    — At least one strong marriage alliance
    — Familiarity with governing: ideally a county to manage before inheriting
      the duchy
  Current risk: No hook on Břetislav; the relationship is affection-based.
    Given his character (compassionate, honest, generous, charming), this is
    probably sufficient — but the three hooked half-brothers create a
    structural asymmetry. If Šwiétosława or any of her sons develop ambitions,
    the hook leverage could be turned against Břetislav's interests.

[DYNASTIC] Přemyslid realm integration (Saxon bloc)
  The six new Saxon vassals are a net asset in troops but a net liability in
  loyalty. Long-term, their integration into the Přemyslid orbit requires:
    — Marriages between their children and Přemyslid court members (over 1-2 gen.)
    — A stable succession (Gavelkind would fracture the realm on Vratislav's death;
      the Saxon counts would pull apart cleanly along cultural lines)
    — No further military humiliations from Bohemia's side (the -25 on Otto is a
      long-standing wound; don't reopen it)

[DYNASTIC] Claim title of King of Bohemia (decided — saving toward this)
  Decision: Save gold to claim the Kingdom of Bohemia title. This is not the
    same as the historical imperial-grant path — it is the CK3 mechanic of
    spending gold/prestige to create (or claim) a kingdom-tier title that we
    already have de jure grounds for.
  Strategic rationale: King-tier status unlocks peer-king alliances. As Duke of
    Bohemia, our alliance pool is limited to other dukes and below; as King,
    neighboring kings (Hungary, Poland, Denmark) become eligible alliance partners.
    Given that we currently have zero alliances and are absorbing a potentially
    unstable Saxon bloc, access to peer-king alliances is a significant upgrade.
  Historical context: Vratislav II was elevated to King of Bohemia in 1085
    (historically, via imperial grant). Playtest 1 achieved this by 1083.
    The two paths — imperial grant vs. title creation — have different prerequisites:
      — Imperial grant: requires sustained good standing with Heinrich (blocked
        by current -20 disapproving opinion; fixing this is the prerequisite path)
      — Title creation: requires gold and prestige; check exact cost in-game
  Current obstacle (shared by both paths): The -20 disapproving opinion of
    Heinrich is a relationship debt with unclear origin. Resolving it unblocks
    the imperial route; if the title-creation route is cheaper/faster, it may
    be independent of the Emperor relationship (verify in-game).
  Papal consideration: Jan of Opava hosting Jaromír complicates papal channels.
    A bishop hosting the duke's rival is an awkward intermediary to Rome for
    either path that involves papal recognition.
  Active obligation: Begin accumulating gold now; do not spend reserves on
    non-critical expenditures when the kingdom claim is on the horizon.

[DYNASTIC] Succession law reform
  Still the generational question: escape Gavelkind before Vratislav dies.
  The Saxon vassal bloc complicates this — their consent becomes necessary,
  and their loyalty is the weakest in the realm. Timing this right (when the
  bloc is sufficiently integrated) is critical.

IV. GRAND AMBITIONS
================================================================

[DYNASTIC] Přemyslid Bohemia: Stability Through Character
  Unchanged from 1066 statement: the goal is to hand Břetislav a realm in
  better shape than it began, with a court culture of trust rather than fear.
  Břetislav's trait profile (compassionate, honest, generous, charming) makes
  him the best possible custodian of this goal if his education completes well.
  The counterweight is the court's current intrigue layer: Záviš (Intrigue 15,
  friend of Šwiétosława), Jaromír (Intrigue 8, embedded in Jan's court),
  Bořivoj (Intrigue focus, greedy+trusting). A reign of virtue requires that
  these currents not overwhelm the heir before he is old enough to navigate them.

[DYNASTIC] The Polish Relationship
  Šwiétosława's children carry Polish blood. A succession to Bolesław III Piast
  has occurred; the relationship with Poland needs re-evaluation under the new
  ruler. The Lechitic language Bořivoj speaks could be an unexpected asset or
  liability depending on how Polish politics develop.

[FOREIGN] The German Question
  Bohemia has made a large bet on the Saxon corridor. If it holds, Bohemia is
  a significant Central European power by the 1080s. If it fractures, the realm
  loses more vassal strength than it started with. Managing the Saxon bloc while
  maintaining HRE good standing is the defining foreign policy question of the
  next decade.
