  Who: Břetislav (id 38159), the probable heir
  Goal: By the time Břetislav inherits (whenever that is), he should have:
       — Diplomacy education tier 3–4

        particularly from Šwiétosława's sons making claims — the
        relationship between father and heir rests on affection and
        structural position rather than leverage. This is probably fine
        given Břetislav's character, but watch for pressure from the
        Boleslav line as the boys grow.

IV. GRAND AMBITIONS  (generational / beyond Vratislav's lifetime)
================================================================

[DYNASTIC] Přemyslid Bohemia: Stability Through Character
  The defining theme of this playtest is narrative over optimization.
  The dynasty's long-term health depends not just on territory and troops
  but on the character of the people who inherit it.
  Břetislav — compassionate, honest, generous, charming — represents the
  best possible successor: a ruler his people will want to follow. If his
  education and marriage are well-managed, he sets a high baseline for
  the next generation. The goal is not just to survive Vratislav's reign
  but to hand Břetislav a realm in better shape than it began, with a
  court culture of trust rather than fear.

[DYNASTIC] Royal Elevation
  Historical note: Vratislav II was elevated to King of Bohemia in 1085
  (as a personal title, not hereditary). In Playtest 1 this had already
  occurred by 1083. Achieving the royal title — whether through the Holy
  Roman Emperor's grant or through pressing a claim — is a long-term
  ambition that reframes the dynasty's standing in Central Europe.
  The papal relationship matters here (Imperial politics and papal
  recognition intertwine). Jan of Opava as a loyal ecclesiastical ally
  gives Bohemia a voice in church affairs.

[DYNASTIC] The Succession Question — Beyond Gavelkind
  If Bohemia operates under Gavelkind in 1066, each succession fractures
  the realm. The long-term ambition is to secure a more stable succession
  law (primogeniture or at minimum seniority) before Vratislav dies.
  This is generational work: it requires vassal consent or a period of
  political dominance that makes resistance futile.
  Břetislav is the right heir to unite the realm after a consolidation.
  Boleslav (diligent, capable) can be a pillar under him — a trusted
  count-cousin rather than a rival.
  Bořivoj is the variable. Watch him.

[FOREIGN] The Polish Relationship
  Šwiétosława represents the Piast connection. Her children (Boleslav,
  Bořivoj, Vladislav) are partly Piast by blood. Whether this is a
  political asset or a complication depends on how Polish politics
  develop. If Poland fractures or becomes hostile, Šwiétosława's sons
  could become vectors of outside influence. If Poland is a stable ally,
  the connection is a genuine strategic asset.
  Long-term aspiration: The Přemyslid-Piast relationship as a durable
  Central European partnership, anchored by Šwiétosława and potentially
  extended through a second marriage alliance (Judita to a Polish lord,
  or a future Přemyslid son to a Piast daughter).

[FOREIGN] The German Question
  Bohemia sits as a vassal of the Holy Roman Empire. The long arc of
  Přemyslid politics involves managing this relationship — extracting
  the royal crown, maintaining autonomy, avoiding being absorbed by
  a dominant German dynasty. Every alliance built westward strengthens
  the negotiating position. Every marriage into a German house is both
  a diplomatic bond and a potential future claim.
  Judita's marriage in ~1072 may be the first deliberate move in this
  long game.
