II. THE OTTO MARRIAGE PROPOSAL: PROS AND CONS
----------------------------------------------

The proposed match:  Bretislav (12) betrothed to Oda (14, Otto's eldest daughter).

Pros:

1. Inheritance angle is stronger than it first appears.
   Otto has NO sons. Oda is his eldest unbetrothed daughter. Adelheid has a disease
   trait and is a poor hereditary risk. Kunigunde is already spoken for. Under most
   succession laws, Oda is the heir presumptive to Meissen + Dresden (two counties,
   five baronies, significant levy). If Oda marries Bretislav and inherits Otto's
   counties, those titles remain within a Bohemian-allied house and could pass in
   turn to Bretislav and Oda's children. This is a realistic path to folding Meissen
   + Dresden into the Přemyslid sphere over one generation.

2. Direct political dividend: immediate.
   As the faction's co-leader, Otto is the primary target. Betrothal ties his family
   to Bratislav's succession — he would be investing in the stability of the Přemyslid
   line rather than undermining it. A betrothal (even before the marriage) typically
   generates significant goodwill and provides grounds to detach him from the faction.

3. Oda's character is usable.
   Diplomacy 6, intrigue 6, learning 7 is a solid consort profile. The deceitful +
   cynical traits echo her father's character and are a legitimate concern for trait
   inheritance — but compassionate partially offsets them, and 'curious' is a positive.
   She is not an exceptional match on raw stats, but she is competent.

4. The age gap works in your favor.
   Oda is 14 now; Bretislav is 12. A betrothal can be arranged immediately. They can
   marry in ~2 years (when Bretislav turns 14 in 1072). No long waiting period.

Cons:

1. Social tier discrepancy.
   Bretislav is heir to the Kingdom of Bohemia. Oda is the daughter of a count. In
   the CK3 prestige economy and in actual 11th-century politics, this is a step down.
   Marrying the heir of a king to a count's daughter when king-level and duke-level
   alternatives exist sends a signal about Bohemia's relative standing. This is a real
   cost even if the pragmatic logic is sound.

2. Deceitful + cynical trait inheritance risk.
   Both Oda and her father share the same negative pair of traits. Their children have
   some probability of inheriting them. Bretislav's own trait profile (compassionate,
   honest, generous, charming) is unusually clean — potentially worth pairing with a
   partner who brings fewer genetic risks.

3. It only addresses Otto, not Ludwig.
   The claimant faction has two members. Betrothing Oda to Bretislav solves Otto;
   Ludwig remains in the faction unless separately handled. See Section IV below for
   the Ludwig situation, which has its own natural dynamics.

4. Otto's stewardship-7 / intrigue-5 makes him a capable schemer regardless.
   A betrothal improves his opinion but does not bind him with hooks. He is a schemer
   by trait and education. Watch for follow-on intrigue activity even after the
   faction question settles.


III. ALTERNATIVE MATCHES FOR BRETISLAV
---------------------------------------

No Catholic king-level daughters are available in the current gamestate (the only
king daughters found are Orthodox: Yevpraxia Rurikid of Ruthenia and Zoe Doukas of
Byzantium). Among viable candidates:

A. Mechthild von Nordheim (id 38614, age 10)
   Father: Otto von Nordheim, Duke of Bavaria (one of the most powerful HRE duchies)
   Skills:  diplomacy 5 | intrigue 4 | learning 2 (modest but developing)
   Traits:  greedy, curious
   Prestige value: HIGH — Duke of Bavaria is a far better father-in-law than Count of
   Meissen. Otto von Nordheim is also emerging as a leader of the Saxon opposition to
   Heinrich IV; in the unfolding Investiture crisis this is a politically significant
   connection.
   Downsides: age 10 (two years younger than Bretislav; betrothal now, marriage ~1074
   vs. Oda's ~1072), 'greedy' is a character flaw, and the marriage provides no
   immediate faction dividend.
   Verdict: the better prestige match if your goal is dynastic elevation; the worse
   tactical match if your goal is stabilising the German counties.

B. Yevpraxia Rurikid (id 38693, age 10)
   Father: Iziaslav, King of Ruthenia (East Slavic, Orthodox)
   Skills:  stewardship 4, prowess 4 (otherwise mediocre)
   Traits:  generous, pensive
   Faith: Orthodox — a faith mismatch with Catholic Bohemia. Bretislav would need to
   get a papal dispensation, or the marriage would generate ongoing opinion penalties
   with the Church (which you can ill afford given the current -30 papal opinion).
   Only worth considering if a Polish/Ruthenian diplomatic connection is specifically
   prioritised over all else.
   Verdict: not recommended given current papal friction.

C. Zoe Doukas (id 38882, age 8)
   Father: Emperor Konstantinos of Byzantium (enormous prestige, Orthodox)
   Traits include: consumption (a serious illness trait).
   Even setting aside the faith mismatch, consumption is a red flag for both life
   expectancy and trait inheritance. Not recommended.

D. Uta Ludowinger (id 37044, age 19) — Ludwig's daughter
   Unbetrothed, good intrigue (7) and learning (6), but vengeful + callous + content.
   A 7-year age gap (19 vs 12) is large; she would need to wait 2 years for Bretislav
   to reach marriage age, and she might not be available then. However — betrothing
   Bretislav to Ludwig's daughter would simultaneously address BOTH faction members
   in a single marriage. The tactical elegance is real.
   Downsides: 'vengeful' is a very negative trait for a queen; 'callous' compounds it.
   The character profile is genuinely difficult. Not recommended as the primary option,
   but worth considering if both factional relationships need to be addressed at once.

Summary of alternatives:
  Best prestige match: Mechthild von Nordheim (Bavaria)
  Best tactical/inheritance match: Oda von Weimar (Meissen)
  Two-birds-one-stone option: Uta Ludowinger (addresses both factions, but poor traits)
  Avoid: Yevpraxia Rurikid (faith mismatch + papal problem), Zoe Doukas (ill)


IV. HANDLING THE FACTION: OTHER TOOLS
---------------------------------------

The marriage is not the only route to neutralising the claimant faction.

A. The Ludwig problem has a natural expiry date.
   Ludwig Ludowinger (the elder, id 29729) has the 'faltering_heart' trait — a
   terminal cardiac condition. He is 55 years old. His prognosis is poor. If he dies
   before the faction reaches war threshold, his heir is Ludwig jr (age 28, already
   married), who would inherit the three Schmalkalden counties but presumably without
   the same grievance or faction standing. The faction likely collapses or resets on
   succession. This makes Ludwig a lower-priority problem than Otto — and suggests
   you may only need to resolve one of the two faction members proactively.

B. Sway schemes on Otto.
   The sway scheme was used twice on Česlav Vrsovid, generating +50 in opinion and
   a passive faction-deterrent. Otto has intrigue 5, which is moderate but not high;
   his deceitful trait reduces the success rate somewhat, but two sway attempts is
   still a realistic path to +40-50 opinion improvement. A council position (Steward
   — he has stewardship 7) adds another large opinion bonus. Combined with a betrothal
   these stack efficiently.

C. Crush the faction militarily.
   Ludwig (1,006 troops) + Otto (857) = 1,863 combined.
   Bohemia alone: 4,802. Plus Poland (2,981 allied). The math is extremely
   comfortable. A quick war would remove both members from the game as faction threats
   and could result in revocation of one or both sets of counties. This is the most
   permanent solution. Costs: general vassal opinion penalty across the realm (~−15
   to −20 for "crushed faction"), which may seed future factions. Currently, with most
   Bohemian vassals at good opinion (Česlav +80, Konrád +30, Ota +30), you have
   headroom to absorb this cost.

D. Give Otto a council position.
   Otto's stewardship 7 makes him a natural fit as Steward. A council seat is a
   direct, substantial opinion boost and costs nothing except the seat itself.
   Combined with a sway scheme this could detach him from the faction before it
   reaches war threshold without requiring a betrothal at all.

E. Address the claimant directly.
   A claimant faction requires a pretender with a claim. Identify who has the claim
   being pressed (the evaluator shows 'demand: unknown' — worth investigating in-game).
   If the claimant holds no titles, they can often be neutralised through a hook or
   imprisoned; without a claimant the faction dissolves. This is the surgical option
   if the claimant is findable and vulnerable.


V. RECOMMENDATION
------------------

The marriage case for Oda is solid but not decisive. The inheritance angle (no sons,
Oda as heir presumptive to two counties) is the strongest argument — it converts a
liability into a long-term asset. The prestige deficit is real but manageable for a
newly elevated king consolidating a German expansion; the more pressing question is
whether Bretislav's marriage should be held in reserve for a better offer.

The cleaner two-step approach:
  1. Neutralise Otto NOW via sway scheme + council seat (Steward). This costs nothing
     except time and is faster than waiting for a betrothal to move opinion. Use the
     breathing room to assess whether a better match appears in the next year or two.
  2. Hold the Oda betrothal as a follow-on once opinion is improved but before Otto
     stabilises in another faction. The betrothal then cements the relationship rather
     than rescuing it from crisis.
  3. For Ludwig: monitor his health. If faltering_heart claims him within the next few
     years, the faction half-solves itself. If he is still active at the 1073 mark,
     use a sway scheme or grant him a council position.

If you prefer a single clean move: the Oda betrothal IS that move, and the inheritance
logic is sound. Just do not expect it to feel like a prestigious match — it is a
tactical marriage dressed in dynastic clothing. Keep Mechthild von Nordheim (Bavaria)
in mind for a future Přemyslid son; that connection has longer legs diplomatically.

For the claimant faction specifically: identify the pretender behind the demand. If
they are neutralisable directly, that is faster than any of the above.
