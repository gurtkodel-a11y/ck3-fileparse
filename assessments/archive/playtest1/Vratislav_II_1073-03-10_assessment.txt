III. THE IMPERIAL RELATIONSHIP AND INVESTITURE CONTEXT
-------------------------------------------------------

Vratislav has insulted Emperor Heinrich IV (-50 insult_opinion: You → Others).
  This is the most diplomatically sensitive development in the period. Heinrich IV
  (age 23, id 37020) is Vratislav's liege. An insult event at -50 is significant
  and lingers roughly 10 years. The cause is not fully determinable from the save,
  but given the timing (post-Lausitz/Meissen expansion), it may reflect friction
  over Bohemia's aggressive territorial expansion within the empire, or a specific
  political dispute.

  The historically informed context makes this more complex: Heinrich IV is at the
  beginning of his great crisis. In 1073 he is facing the Saxon revolt. By 1076 he
  will be at open war with Pope Gregory VII (the Investiture Controversy). His need
  for reliable vassal support will intensify sharply over the next three years.
  Historically, Vratislav II was Heinrich IV's most loyal supporter during the
  Investiture era, and was rewarded with the royal crown in 1085 (Bohemia already
  has the crown here — likely already awarded as a result of early loyalty, which
  makes the subsequent insult confusing).

  The insult is a vulnerability. If Heinrich demands military support in the coming
  Saxon conflict, Vratislav cannot refuse without making the relationship worse. But
  participating loyally in Heinrich's campaigns would be the fastest repair mechanism
  — and historically accurate.

The Pope (-30 impious opinion, persisting since ~1070):
  The papal relationship has not improved in two and a half years. An impious
  opinion suggests alignment with the Emperor against Church prerogatives — probably
  related to the Meissen/German expansion, which the Pope may have viewed as
  conducted outside proper canonical channels.

  The coming Investiture showdown places Bohemia in an awkward position: Vratislav
  has insulted the Emperor AND angered the Pope. This is the worst of both worlds.
  The strategic question is which relationship to prioritise repairing. Given that
  Bohemia already has the royal crown (Heinrich's gift), the Emperor is the more
  important partner going forward — but being excommunicated by the Pope would create
  severe domestic opinion penalties and prevent marriages. Monitoring both is essential.

V. PRIORITIES: SHORT, MEDIUM, LONG TERM
-----------------------------------------

SHORT-TERM (next 1-2 years, by ~1075):

  1. Reinstate Česlav Vrsovid to the council.
     His faction membership is directly traceable to the council firing (-20). He
     has low diplomacy (1) and low intrigue (1) — he is not a schemer by nature;
     he reacts emotionally (arbitrary trait) to slights. Putting him back on the
     council reverses the -20 and likely detaches him from the faction, leaving
     Ludwig alone. Ludwig alone (1,067 troops) is not a meaningful threat and will
     not survive indefinitely with faltering_heart.

  2. Grant Svatobor a holding in d_meissen.
     He and Oda are both still in Vratislav's court. For the dynastic plan to
     function — Oda inheriting Meissen through Svatobor's line — Svatobor needs to
     be established as a landed Přemyslid within the duchy. A single county or barony
     from d_meissen gives him standing, removes him from the court head count, and
     begins the process of normalising a Přemyslid presence in the German holdings.

  3. Begin developing Čáslav.
     Seven years of delay. The special economic site gives above-average development
     returns. Every year of inaction is a compounding loss. This is the lowest-cost
     action on this list and should have been done in 1067.

  4. Attend to the Emperor.
     The insult to Heinrich IV needs a repair plan. The Saxon revolt is beginning
     now (1073-1075 historically). Participating in Heinrich's campaign — even with
     a single levy contribution — is the fastest path from -50 to a functional
     imperial relationship, and it is the historically natural move for Bohemia.
     Refusing imperial calls to arms would compound the diplomatic damage.

MEDIUM-TERM (2-5 years, by ~1077-1078):

  5. Marry Břetislav to Mechthild Nordheim (due ~1074).
     He turns 16 in January 1074. She is 13 in 1073 — likely old enough to complete
     the marriage by late 1074 or 1075. This closes the Bavaria alliance as a formal
     dynastic bond and secures the heir's position.

  6. Assess Ludwig Ludowinger's succession.
     Ludwig (age 58, health 3.3, faltering_heart) may die within the next few years.
     His son Ludwig jr (age ~31, already married) holds no titles but will inherit
     the three Schmalkalden counties. Ludwig jr's character — gluttonous, lazy,
     wrathful — makes him liable to be a passive vassal rather than a faction leader.
     If the elder Ludwig dies before the liberty faction reaches war threshold, the
     faction may dissolve without intervention. Monitor.

  7. Navigate the Investiture Controversy.
     The Gregory VII / Heinrich IV showdown arrives ~1076. Bohemia's choice:
     — Support Heinrich (historically correct, already begun): maintains imperial
       relationship, risks papal excommunication, but Heinrich controls the path to
       further German titles and imperial legitimacy.
     — Side with the Pope: repairs the -30 impious penalty, but alienates the liege
       and may forfeit imperial protections.
     — Neutral opportunism: difficult to execute; both sides will demand commitment.
     Given that Bohemia's royal crown came from Heinrich and the German vassal base
     is already established, continuing to support the Emperor is the rational choice.
     Plan for a temporary excommunication and its domestic opinion costs.

  8. Plan the northern campaign.
     The preconditions are nearly met: Poland (allied), Bavaria (allied), Styria
     (allied), realm stabilised. The trigger condition is Catholic fervor above 50
     (already at 43 and rising) and a declared holy war CB on Pomerania. The
     practical timing: resolve the liberty faction, complete Břetislav's marriage,
     then declare. A joint campaign with Poland (who has its own interest in
     Pomeranian territory) would be the optimal execution.

  9. Position younger sons.
     Boleslav (7), Bořivoj (6), Vladislav (5), Albrecht (2), and Mlada (infant girl)
     are all unlanded. As they mature, giving them titles — particularly from any
     pagan holy war gains — channels their ambition outward rather than inward.
     A landless Přemyslid cadet at 25 becomes a faction risk; one with a county does
     not. Start planning land grants for the eldest (Boleslav) around 1080.

LONG-TERM (5+ years, by ~1085 and beyond):

  10. Consolidate d_meissen as a Přemyslid hereditary holding.
      When Otto dies (he is 46 and holds no alarming health traits), d_meissen passes
      to Oda, and from Oda to her Přemyslid children with Svatobor. At that point the
      duchy can be reorganised as a Přemyslid cadet holding under the Bohemian crown.
      No action required now beyond establishing Svatobor.

  11. Extend Czech cultural influence.
      Czech culture holds 42 titles — flat since 1066. Franconian (160), Saxon (119),
      Hungarian (116), Russian (135) all show in the local zone. As Bohemia incorporates
      German territories (Meissen, Lausitz), the cultural question becomes whether to
      convert vassal lands or manage multi-cultural complexity. The Hard Working
      tradition's development bonus gives an incentive to develop rather than convert.
      No urgency here, but a long-term consideration.

  12. Eventual kingdom elevation or peer recognition.
      Bohemia is already a kingdom. The next ambition tier is either:
      — Expanding the kingdom's de jure claim structure (absorbing more de jure
        territory into k_bohemia's claim hierarchy)
      — A title created from the German acquisitions (Meissen + Lausitz could, with
        further additions, form a Lusatian or Meissen kingdom tier)
      — Or simply maximising the existing kingdom's development and military dominance
        without further formal elevation.
      No immediate action needed, but the architecture for either path should be
      considered as territory consolidates.

Bottom line (1073):
  Bohemia is in its strongest position since the game began. The realm has grown,
  the alliance network is formidable, the two most dangerous vassals have been
  converted into the two most loyal ones, and the heir is on track. The vulnerabilities
  are diplomatic (insult to the Emperor, papal friction) and developmental (domain
  stagnation). The next crisis is the Investiture Controversy, which arrives within
  three years and will demand a clear strategic position. Everything before that
  should be oriented toward entering that crisis from the strongest possible
  domestic and alliance position.
