I. THE REALM: HOLDINGS, VASSALS, AND INTERNAL BALANCE OF POWER
---------------------------------------------------------------

one farmlands county (Litomeřice) provides agricultural wealth, one mountains
county (Hradec) anchors the northeast, and two plains counties (Boleslav,
Plzeň) round out the west. The special economic site at Čáslav (poi_special
economic building) is a development asset to lean on early. Full control
everywhere and an average development of 8.6 per holding puts Bohemia in a
healthy economic position for a starting duchy.

The vassal picture divides cleanly into three tiers:

Tier 1 — The Přemyslid cousins (Moravia):
  Konrád Přemyslid (Count of Brno) is by far the most powerful vassal, with

  1,402 combined troops. His combat power is 34,500 — roughly a third of
  Vratislav's own 91,580. Ota Přemyslid (Count of Olomouc) adds another 825
  troops across two holdings. The two Přemyslids together control a contiguous
  Moravian block in the east that represents the dominant internal power
  concentration, and as family members they have the dynastic standing to act
  on it if relations sour.

Tier 2 — The problematic vassals:
  Jaromír Přemyslid (Count of Opava, theocracy vassal) is Vratislav's declared
  mutual rival. He holds 641 levy and 1,152 total troops in a forested border
  county pointing northeast toward Silesia. As a theocracy vassal he cannot be
  revoked through normal channels, and as a sitting rival he generates a steady
  opinion drain. He is politically isolated — a bishop with no secular faction
  base — but his rivalry is a live wound.

  Česlav Vrsovid (Count of Žatec) is the only non-Přemyslid feudal count,
  holding a single farmlands county in the fertile northwest. The Vrsovids are
  a rival dynasty historically; his 260 levy is negligible today but his
  independence from the Přemyslid house makes him a potential faction nucleus
  long-term.

Tier 3 — The minor barons:
  Eight republic-type baron vassals (Beroun, Bílina, Bechyně, Děčín, Domažlice,
  Jaroměř, Milevsko, Vraclav, Vyšehrad) contribute minimal individual levy
  (~70–72 each) and are politically negligible. They represent the commercial
  and ecclesiastical fabric of Bohemia rather than military weight.

House-head position: Vratislav holds house-head hooks on Boleslav, Bořivoj,
and Vladislav — three of his six children. These are succession insurance, not
today's tools.

Overall internal assessment: stable, controlled, and carrying one live
diplomatic wound (Jaromír) and one medium-term structural risk (Konrád's
relative weight in Moravia).


II. MILITARY POSTURE AND CULTURAL SYNERGY
------------------------------------------

Realm strength at opening is 3,292 maximum (2,467 levy + 825 men-at-arms),
all currently unraised. The MaA composition is well-suited to Bohemia's terrain:

  zbrojnosh (Heavy Infantry, size 500) — the Czech national unit, with bonuses
    of +8 damage on hills and +6 on both mountains and forest. Praha, Čáslav,
    and Doudleby are all hills counties; the northeastern border runs through
    mountains and forest. This regiment will fight at its best on home ground.
  Light Horsemen (size 200) — effective on plains and drylands; useful for
    offensive projection onto Poland or Hungary's flatter terrain, but penalized
    on the mountainous borders.
  Pikemen (size 100) — steady heavy-footline support, no terrain penalties.
  Mangonel (size 20) — siege capacity; small but present from day one.

The cultural traditions compound the terrain advantage:
  Mountaineer Ruralism: defensive bonuses in hills and mountains — exactly the
    terrain of Bohemia's interior and northern borders.
  Castle Keepers: improved castle defenses across the domain.
  Hard Working: development growth bonus, amplifying the existing dev base.

Five knights (Kunrát prowess 10, Petr 10, Lanź 9, Ota 8, Česlav 6) give a
moderate heavy-cavalry pool. Prowess 10 is strong but the bench is thin; Lanź
and Ota fill out the list decently.

The net military picture: Bohemia punches above its levy numbers in defensive
engagements thanks to terrain, traditions, and the zbrojnosh. For offense —
particularly any campaign toward the pagan north — the force composition is
solid but unspectacular. The siege train is minimal; no sustained campaign
against fortified opponents is advisable without expansion.

IV. THREATS, OPPORTUNITIES, AND PRIORITIES
-------------------------------------------

Immediate threats (internal):
  1. Jaromír Přemyslid (rival, Count of Opava) — the single most damaging
     relationship in the realm. He cannot be revoked, he cannot be liked, and
     his border county creates a northeastern salient that is both strategically
     exposed and an opinion drain. Near-term handling options: imprison on a
     pretext, seek a hook to neutralize him, or simply endure and plan for the
     long game as he ages.

  2. Konrád of Brno — not hostile, but the levy ratio (~40% of Vratislav's own)
     is approaching the threshold where faction leadership becomes plausible as
     opinions fluctuate or succession becomes uncertain. Keeping him well-treated
     and cross-married into Vratislav's children is the best insurance.

Medium-term external threats:
  3. Poland — Bolesław Piast has a real army, no current alliance with Bohemia,
     and historical interest in territories that overlap with Bohemia's zone.
     This is the primary external uncertainty at game start.

  4. HRE obligations — as the empire heads toward Investiture turbulence,
     Vratislav will face choices about which side to support. The Papal alliance
     is implicit given Czech Catholic traditions (Armed Pilgrimages, strong
     Church doctrines), but playing both sides has historically generated large
     rewards in this era.

  5. Pagan northern neighbors — not immediately threatening, but the combined
     strength of the northern pagan belt exceeds Bohemia's realm total. The
     window for preemptive engagement (before any pagan consolidation) is the
     next few decades.

Opportunities:
  1. Alliance with Hungary — the most natural first diplomatic move. The Árpáds
     are Catholic, adjacent, and currently at peace. A marriage of a Přemyslid
     child to an Árpád minor would cost nothing and gain a significant military
     partner.

  2. Holy war northward — Slavic pagans along the northern border are legitimate
     targets under Catholicism's armed pilgrimages tenet. The Pomeranian and
     Lutician duchies represent territorial upside; timing depends on when the
     HRE's distraction with Saxony creates political cover.

  3. Čáslav special economic site — development investment here returns above
     average income relative to flat-development counties. A strong early
     building choice.

  4. The zbrojnosh advantage — once Bohemia is in a defensive war on home
     terrain, the zbrojnosh + Mountaineer Ruralism + Castle Keepers combination
     creates a materially favorable defensive engagement profile. Playing for
     defensive wars (letting enemies come to the hills) is a legitimate early
     strategy.

Bottom line:
  Day one of a solid starting position. No crises, full control, viable military
  for the region, and cultural traditions well-aligned with the terrain. The
  vulnerabilities are diplomatic: zero alliances and one live internal rival.
  First priorities are clear — resolve the Jaromír problem by whatever means
  presents itself, and open alliance negotiations with Hungary before Poland or
  a pagan neighbor creates a more complicated situation. The Investiture era
  begins immediately and will demand attention; staying in Heinrich's good graces
  while the empire is strong is cheap insurance that quickly becomes expensive
  to purchase later.
