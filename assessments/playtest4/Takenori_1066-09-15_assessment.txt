I. THE REALM: HOLDINGS, VASSALS, AND INTERNAL BALANCE OF POWER
---------------------------------------------------------------

Takenori holds the Duchy of Mutsu in name across six titles — the duchy seat,
two real counties (Isawa, Taga), the two baronies that anchor them, and a
sixth title, "the Kiyohara Family," which is not a landholding at all but an
honorary clan-headship title (it carries no barony and isn't counted among
his domain holdings). His actual physical domain is just two castle holdings:
Isawa (development 8, hills, fort 7, a noted martial site) and Taga
(development 7, hills, fort 8, a noted economic site, also his capital and
current location). Combined development is a modest 15, average 7.5 — by the
Steward's own assessment, low, with three whole building categories absent
(military, religious, urban). This is a duke who governs lightly: two
holdings, one direct vassal.

That one vassal, Arimitsu Ishikawa, Count of Iwaki, fields 296 levy / 449
total troops under a "Japan Feudal Vassal" contract and is currently neutral
— no factions, no recorded hostility. Iwaki's own domain (1 holding, dev 9,
hills) is in fact slightly better developed than either of Takenori's own
holdings, a small but real asymmetry worth watching even though nothing
about it looks urgent today.

The real structure of Takenori's power isn't vassal count — it's family.
As dynasty head of House Kiyohara (founded 735.1.1, prestige 926), he holds
house-head hooks on twelve people: Mitsuyori, Mitsuko, two different
Takemichis, Takesada, Takehira, Yoshiko 2, Yoritó, Sanehira 3, Noriko 3,
Sadako, and Iehira. That is, functionally, his entire extended clan under
direct personal leverage. The Spymaster's brief calls this "a strong
position for coercion and compliance," and it shows in the numbers: no
active factions, broadly strong vassal loyalty, and a court that is itself
mostly family — his wife Kiyoko and two of his sons (Takehira, Takemichi)
sit on his council alongside two non-family retainers (Koreyori, Yoritó).
This is a clan government in the literal sense, not just the cultural one.

Military quality is mixed. The knight roster (7 total) splits sharply: three
high-prowess fighters (Yoritó and Arimitsu at 10, Mitsuyori at 9) alongside
two more at 8 (both named Takemichi/Takehira — likely his own sons) and two
at prowess 0 (Koreyori, and a second Takemichi) who are probably either very
young or simply unbloodied. Average prowess (6.4) is, per the Marshal,
below par. The four men-at-arms regiments are conventional for the region —
horse archers, heavy footmen, a skirmisher block, and a siege unit — with no
glaring gaps, but capacity (467 MaA cap, 731 levy, 1,198 total max) is small
in absolute terms.


II. THE EXTERNAL SITUATION
---------------------------

Takenori is a sworn vassal of Yorimichi, Emperor of Japan (4,505 troops,
117,520 power) — Takenori's own strength is about 27% of his liege's, a
proportionate, unremarkable position for a ducal vassal, not a red flag on
its own. His faith, pundarika (a Buddhist tradition, Eastern/Dharmic family,
83 titles held), has no recognized religious head at this date — the
Councilor's brief flags this directly as a loss of doctrinal authority and
piety generation, worth keeping an eye on as a long-run lever (sponsoring or
backing a head claimant, if that becomes available, would be a real gain).

Diplomatically, Takenori has exactly one alliance: Hidetake Kimiko, Count of
Dewa, bonded through the marriage of Takenori's daughter Yoshiko 2 to
Hidetake. One bond is a thin diplomatic base — both the Marshal and
Chancellor independently flag this as the central external vulnerability:
a single alliance breaking, or Hidetake simply dying without the bond being
renewed through the next generation, would leave Takenori isolated.

A genuine and important caveat on "nearby" rulers: the tool's geographic
zone classification returned over 1,500 rulers as "local/nearby" to
Takenori, including the Emperor of Great Liao, the King of Maghreb (North
Africa), the Emperor of Cumania (Caspian steppe), and the King of Tamilakam
(southern India). These are not realistic threats or contacts for a Duke of
Mutsu in northern Honshu in 1066 — this is almost certainly an artifact of
how the tool's region/zone heuristic (`ck3/geography.py`) handles an island
nation in a save built on the much larger All Under Heaven map; it likely
hasn't been tuned for Japan's actual sea-route isolation. Treat the
"Geographic Neighbors" list as "notable world powers," not "people who can
march on you." The two entries on that list that ARE plausibly real
neighbors — close enough across the Sea of Japan/Korea Strait to matter —
are:
  - Chala Yelú, Emperor of Great Liao (8,649 troops, 347,699 power) — by far
    the largest nearby power, controlling Manchuria/the Liao Empire.
  - Hwi, Emperor of Goryeo (4,724 troops, 110,959 power) — Korea, directly
    across the strait.
Both are unallied with Takenori. The Marshal's top priority — closing the
power gap with Liao via levies, MaA, or alliance — is sound advice even
discounting the zone-classification noise; Liao really is the dominant nearby
empire, war or no war.


III. THREATS, ASSETS, AND PRIORITIES
--------------------------------------

Internal:
  1. Health is poor (2.9) at age 62. This isn't an immediate crisis, but it
     puts succession planning on the near-term clock rather than the
     long-term one. Worth digging into directly next — Takenori's heir,
     Takesada, already rules his own separate realm (Duke of Nushiro)
     rather than living in Takenori's court, which raises a real question
     about how succession and domain unification are meant to play out.
     Flagging for the family-situation conversation we're about to have.
  2. Domain underdevelopment — avg dev 7.5, three building categories
     (military, religious, urban) entirely absent. The Steward's recommended
     first move is developing Taga toward 15 before expanding further.
  3. Thin vassal base — one direct vassal is a small support structure for
     a duke. Not unstable today (no factions, vassal loyalty solid), but it
     means almost all of Takenori's military weight is his own to carry.

External:
  4. Diplomatic isolation — one alliance, fragile by construction (a single
     marriage bond to one family). Both Marshal and Chancellor flag this
     independently; an alliance with a real, geographically plausible power
     (Goryeo is the more reachable target; Liao the more consequential one)
     would do more for Takenori's position than almost anything else
     available right now.
  5. No recognized faith head for pundarika — a slow-burn loss of piety and
     doctrinal standing rather than an urgent problem.
  6. Knight quality is uneven — two knights at prowess 0 drag the average
     down; replacing or training them up is a cheap, concrete improvement.

Assets:
  - Total command of the extended Kiyohara clan via twelve house-head hooks
    — a genuinely strong internal-control position most rulers don't have
    this early.
  - Defensible terrain: both domain holdings are hills, with solid
    fortification levels (7 and 8).
  - No active factions, no recorded rivals or hostile relations of any kind
    — a calm internal picture to build from.

Bottom line:
  Takenori opens as a small, well-controlled duchy riding on the strength of
  family loyalty rather than raw material power. The clan is firmly his —
  twelve hooks, a family-staffed council, zero factions — but the duchy
  itself is thin: two holdings, one vassal, one alliance, middling troops,
  and a heir who currently rules elsewhere. None of this is a crisis. It's
  a foundation that rewards careful building: develop the domain, find a
  second alliance (Goryeo is the realistic near-term target), and get
  ahead of the succession question with Takesada before age and health force
  the issue. The Liao Empire is the one external power worth genuinely
  respecting on the regional chessboard; everything else flagged as "nearby"
  by the tool should be read with real skepticism until the zone-heuristic
  gap for island nations gets fixed.
