Project Ideas — CK3 Fileparse
================================================================

A brainstorming cupboard. Not a roadmap or a to-do list — a place to capture
ideas, observations, and design notes before they're forgotten. Some of these
will get built; others will inform future decisions even if never implemented.

Entries are grouped by theme. Within a theme, each idea has a short pitch and
any relevant notes on feasibility, data availability, or caveats.


================================================================
I. QUERYING / TOOLING GAPS (surfaced by actual use)
================================================================

These are gaps that came up during real assessment work — places where the
save data clearly contains the answer but we couldn't surface it cleanly.

--- War history / event log ---
The single highest-value missing tool. The save almost certainly contains
closed-war records (CK3 tracks war history). A query that surfaces "wars
resolved since date X" — with combatants, outcome, and territorial change —
would transform post-session archaeology into actual history. The 1066→1071
transition has clear fingerprints (opinion modifiers, new vassals) but the
sequence is opaque.
Data source to explore: raw["war"] or similar top-level key; war records may
include end-date, warscore, attacker/defender/target.

--- Betrothal status ---
character_profile() doesn't surface betrothals. Judita being in Regensburg
is strongly suggestive but unconfirmable without a separate query. CK3 tracks
betrothals as a relation type in the save. A betrothal_for(char_id) query
returning (partner_id, arranged_date, marriage_date) would be a small addition
to character_profile() or a standalone helper.
Data source: relations.active_relations, probably relation_type="betrothal".

--- Guardian finder ---
"Find the best available guardian for a child with focus X, within realm Y"
was a recurring need in Playtest 2. We wrote the 1066 plan based on a manual
survey of two candidates and missed Ota Přemyslid entirely (who turned out
to be the obvious right answer). A query: guardian_candidates(gs, char_id,
focus) → ranked list of in-realm characters by education tier + personality
fit for that focus, excluding characters already serving as guardians, would
make this trivially fast.
Note on Jan restriction: In CK3, clerical characters (and likely realm chaplains)
cannot be assigned as guardians even when they are vassals. This is unintuitive
from a narrative perspective (a bishop-scholar is exactly who you'd want
educating a child) but mechanically blocked. Worth flagging in the tool output:
"Jan of Opava (D=8, L=18) — INELIGIBLE: clerical vassal."

--- Character lookup by name or partial profile ---
"Who is Tamar?" and "Who is Arnold?" came up in the 1071 assessment.
Both are characters referenced by opinion modifiers or personal relations but
not further identified. A find_character(gs, name) → list[CharacterProfile]
function would answer these in seconds. Could be a lightweight scan of gs.chars
by decoded first_name.

--- Succession law query ---
The succession law governing the player's realm has been "unknown" across all
Playtest 2 assessments. It affects urgency calculations for inheritance planning
but isn't surfaced by any current evaluator. Should be readable from
landed_data or realm law records.
Data source: probably raw["laws"] or title.succession_law on the primary title.

--- Opinion modifier detail ---
The relationships evaluator surfaces top notable opinions but doesn't decode
the source modifier (only the label). "Tamar: -30 (imprisoned_me)" is useful;
"Tamar: -30 (imprisoned_me — imprisoned 1068.4.1, expires never)" would be
better. Opinion modifier objects likely include timestamp and duration.

--- Knight-who-is-also-vassal flag ---
At 1071.2.5, Benno von Lóningen (Count of Wolkenstein) and Reginar (Count of
Dresden) are both listed as Vratislav's knights AND as county-tier vassals.
This is an unusual dual role that has military and political implications —
a knight who is also a powerful vassal has divided loyalty in a different way
than a household knight. Worth flagging in the military report when it occurs.


================================================================
II. EVALUATOR ENHANCEMENTS
================================================================

--- Economy evaluator ---
Noted as a major gap in CLAUDE.md. Gold income, building yields, development
return — the third CK3 pillar alongside military and diplomacy. The terrain
evaluator has building data; an economy evaluator would need income calculations
from landed titles and buildings.

--- Opinion scores (vassal loyalty) ---
raw["opinions"] is unexplored. Surfacing which vassals like/dislike the player
and why — especially the new Saxon bloc with uncertain loyalty — would add real
value to the relationships report. The faction risk section is currently
qualitative; with opinion scores it becomes quantitative.

--- Sub-vassal / realm tree ---
Current relationships report shows only direct vassals. A realm-tree view —
direct vassals, their direct vassals, total indirect levy — would give a fuller
picture of the political structure. Especially relevant for larger realms.

--- Faction tracker ---
Factions are surfaced by the relationships evaluator but haven't been validated
for Playtest 2 (Murchad had none at the test date). When the Saxon vassal bloc
starts forming opinions, faction tracking will matter. Should surface: faction
type, members, leader, demand, player's ability to satisfy it.

--- Active-war detail ---
MarshalAdvisor note from CLAUDE.md: doesn't yet reference own_armies/enemy_armies
during active wars. Extending this would make the military report much more
useful when a war is actually happening.

--- War-retargeting flag (surfaced by playtest5, Meissen/Lausitz) ---
Documented as a caveat in ck3/evaluators/military.py for now (module docstring
+ ActiveWar dataclass), not yet built as a feature: when you take a title via
conquest, a third party's separate war over that SAME title (e.g. another
claimant who was already fighting the previous holder) doesn't resolve — it
retargets to you as the new holder. Player went from "won the Meissen war" to
"defending against Dedo over Meissen" in one transition with no warning. A
real enhancement would have the Marshal brief proactively flag this: after a
conquest, check active_wars for any war whose targeted_titles overlaps a
title that just changed hands, and call out "this looks like an inherited
war, not one you declared." Needs the title-history-as-war-log approach
(section IV) to know "just changed hands" in the first place. Revisit when
war frequency picks up enough to make this worth automating rather than
catching by hand.

--- Geography zone heuristic breaks down for island nations (found in Playtest 4) ---
ck3/geography.py's region/zone classification returned 1,500+ rulers as
"local/nearby" to Takenori (Duke of Mutsu, Japan), including the Emperor of
Great Liao, the King of Maghreb (North Africa), the Emperor of Cumania
(Caspian steppe), and the King of Tamilakam (southern India). The heuristic
leans on the de jure/kingdom-tier proximity network, which apparently treats
Japan's island position as "close" to a huge swath of the Eurasian landmass
once All Under Heaven's bigger map is in play — sea isolation isn't being
modeled. Realistic neighbors (Goryeo across the strait, Liao via Korea/
Manchuria) get buried in noise alongside genuinely irrelevant continental
rulers. Worth a targeted fix: either model sea-route distance explicitly, or
at minimum cap "local" zone membership by a real proximity check rather than
de jure network reachability alone. Flagged as a DLC-coverage-adjacent gap —
not unique to a specific DLC, but only surfaced because All Under Heaven's
map expansion made the region table's blind spots visible.


================================================================
III. CHARACTER / NARRATIVE TOOLS
================================================================

--- Naming convention: "narrative" is the broader concept, not character-only ---
Course-corrected during playtest5: "narrative" should refer to the full
multi-scale concept below, not be used as a synonym for character-level
events. ck3/evaluators/narrative.py was renamed to character_narrative.py
(run_narrative.py -> run_character_narrative.py) to make this explicit —
it covers the Personal (and some Family) rung specifically, not "narrative"
in general. Keep this distinction in any future module naming.

--- Full narrative evaluator (multi-scale, NOT YET BUILT) ---
The long-term goal: a narrative layer that surfaces events of note at
several nested scales, each wider than the last. Working list of rungs
(player's framing, possibly too fine-grained but captures the gist):
  Personal      — one character's own life beats (built: character_narrative.py)
  Family        — births/deaths/marriages/feuds across a dynasty/house,
                   not just one person's memory of them
  Court         — intrigue, factions, council drama, guardian/ward dynamics
                   within a single ruler's court
  Holdings      — development, building completions, sieges, local unrest
                   at the barony/county level
  Domain        — the player's own realm as a whole: vassal politics,
                   succession, realm laws changing
  Local         — the immediate geographic neighborhood (the zone-0/1
                   rulers Marshal/Chancellor already track) — wars,
                   successions, alliances among neighbors
  Continental   — broader regional shifts: kingdoms rising/falling,
                   religious-fervor swings, large coalition wars
  Global        — empire-tier and world-historical events
Each rung likely wants its own data sources (character_memory_manager for
Personal/Family; landed_titles history + faction_manager for Court/Domain;
the title-history-as-war-log approach from section IV below for Local/
Continental). Not clear yet whether this becomes one evaluator with scale
as a parameter, or a family of small evaluators under a shared narrative/
package the way advisors share Council. Revisit once Court or Domain-scale
data needs come up organically in a playtest, the way Personal did with
Ludmila's death — don't design the other six rungs speculatively in advance
of an actual need.

--- Character narrative (Personal scale) memory-decay workaround (BUILT, follow-up needed) ---
ck3/evaluators/character_narrative.py (life_story()) is built and working — walks
character_memory_manager and renders a character's life as a chronological
narrative (births, deaths, marriages, rivalries, battles, etc.), with a
fallback for dead characters: their own alive_data.memories pointer is gone
once they move to dead_unprunable, so we cross-reference other living
characters' memory lists for entries naming the dead character as a
participant (e.g. a parent's "relative_died" memory) and tag them "(per
{owner}'s memory)". This is how Ludmila's death shows up in her own story
at all — she can't remember it, but her family can.

Known limitation, not yet addressed: CK3 memories carry an end_date and
appear to decay — observed end_dates run ~130 years past creation_date.
That's fine within a living character's own lifetime, but two real gaps:
  1. Once a memory expires, it's presumably removed from the save entirely
     (not yet confirmed — would need a save where an old enough memory has
     actually crossed its end_date to verify). If so, very old events on
     long-lived dynasties/ancestors could quietly vanish from the record.
  2. The dead-character fallback only works as long as at least one living
     character still holds the cross-referencing memory. Once Vratislav,
     Judita, Břetislav, etc. are *also* dead and their own memories expire
     or they're pruned, Ludmila's death becomes unrecoverable from the
     memory system — there'd be no path back to it at all (dead_data still
     has the bare cause-of-death fact, but loses the relational/emotional
     framing the narrative tool adds).
Possible directions to explore later: snapshot life_story() output to a
persistent file per character at the point of death (so the narrative is
captured before memories start decaying), or treat the memory system as a
"hot cache" and maintain our own append-only narrative log across saves
instead of re-deriving it fresh from each save's memory state. Not urgent —
parking here per playtest5 discussion, revisit if/when a save is old enough
for decay to actually start biting.

--- Character arc tracking ---
The most interesting stories in Playtest 2 are about how characters change
over time: Jaromír drifting from "exiled rival" to "embedded schemer at the
friendly bishop's court"; Břetislav developing from a 13-year-old with a crush
into (eventually) a duke; Bořivoj's greedy+trusting trajectory. A tool that
generates a "character arc" by comparing profiles across multiple saves would
make this explicit rather than reconstructed.
Design: character_arc(char_id, [save_path1, save_path2, ...]) → delta-summary
of skills, traits, relationships, titles over time.

--- First-person narrative voice ---
The long-term project vision is an LLM that can engage with the game from
a first-person perspective (Vratislav's point of view, not the strategic
overview). The character_profile() evaluator is the right foundation — it
surfaces everything intrinsic and extrinsic. The next step is a prompt template
that feeds a character profile + relationship context into Claude and asks for
first-person internal monologue or dialogue.
Note: assessments/playtest2/ documents are good seed material for tone and
narrative style. They model the balance between mechanical fact and story.

--- Council scene generator ---
Each council advisor (marshal, chancellor, etc.) produces a brief. A "council
scene" would frame these as in-character dialogue — the Marshal reporting on
a military threat, the Chancellor advising on a vassal problem, the Spymaster
warning about Bořivoj. The advisor layer already exists; this is a rendering
question, not a data question.

--- Relationship web visualization ---
The character web descriptions in assessments are written manually. A tool
that generates a DOT/graphviz relationship diagram from CharacterProfile data
(connecting characters by marriage, friendship, rivalry, guardian/ward,
liege/vassal bonds) would make the structure visible at a glance. Text-mode
approximation possible; proper rendering requires a graphviz step.


================================================================
IV. WORKFLOW / INFRASTRUCTURE
================================================================

--- Title history as war log (KEY FINDING) ---
CK3 confirmed behavior: `wars.active_wars` contains ONLY currently active wars.
No end_date or result field exists. triggered_event is a future queue. Character
records have no history field. Completed wars do NOT persist as war records.

BUT: `landed_titles[id].history` is a `{date: holder_id_or_{type,holder}}` dict
on every title that ever changed hands. When a title is taken by conquest, the
entry is `{type: 'conquest_claim', holder: char_id}` with the exact date.

Verified: d_meissen (title 3453) shows `1070.12.29: {type: conquest_claim, holder: 32778}`.
This directly records that Vratislav took the Duchy of Meissen on 29 Dec 1070 via
a conquest claim. The counties (Meissen, Naumburg, Dresden, etc.) became Bohemian
vassals automatically as de jure subjects of the duchy — no separate conquest needed.

Implication: a "session history" tool can be built from a SINGLE save (no diff needed)
by scanning all titles for history entries since the last-played date:
  — filter landed_titles for history entries where date >= session_start_date
  — group by date: multiple title changes on the same day = likely one war
  — decode transfer type (conquest_claim, usurpation, inheritance, etc.)
  — resolve holder IDs to character names
  — output: "1070.12.29: Vratislav (Bohemia) took d_meissen from Otto (Meissen)
    via conquest claim. De jure vassals affected: [list]"

This is the primary war-history tool. Other signals (opinion modifiers, vassal
roster) are detective-work fallbacks for cases not captured by title changes
(e.g., wars that resulted in gold/prestige rather than title transfer).

Priority: HIGH — data is confirmed available, tool design is clear.

--- Autosave mid-session monitoring ---
Complementary to the title-history approach above: parsing autosaves while a war
is active would capture it in active_wars before it resolves, giving combatant
detail and battle results that title history doesn't store.
CK3 keeps 2-3 autosave rotation files. Limitation: a war that starts and ends
between two autosave points is still invisible to this method (but would appear
in title history). Full parsing is slow (~3 min per 96MB save); mitigate by
parsing only the wars section.
Best use: "what war is Bratislav currently fighting and how is it going?" —
a mid-session check rather than retrospective reconstruction.

--- Playtest assessment template ---
The assessments in assessments/playtest2/ evolved organically. A template
(with standard sections for position / character web / open questions / tooling
gaps) would make them faster to write and more consistent to compare across
save dates. Could be a Markdown template file.

--- Guardian restriction catalog ---
Following the Jan of Opava finding: CK3 has a number of non-obvious restrictions
on who can serve as a guardian (clerical characters, realm chaplains, possibly
imprisoned characters, possibly characters at foreign courts). Building a catalog
of these restrictions — derived from game files or from empirical testing —
would improve the guardian_candidates() tool and prevent planning on ineligible
candidates.

--- Save file comparison infrastructure ---
We have 16+ extracted saves across Playtest 1 and Playtest 2. Most are too large
to keep in memory simultaneously. A lightweight index (date, char_id, realm size,
key stats) built from each save without loading the full parse would enable
cross-save queries without the 2-minute parse overhead.

--- Git-backed save history per playtest (META) ---
Idea: keep each playtest's unpacked saves in their own little git repo (one per
playtest, gitignored from the main repo) so the sequence of snapshots becomes a
real commit history — git log as a timeline, git diff between two dates as a
free "what changed" query, and git branches as a way to fork off "what if"
experiments from a given snapshot.
Appeal: snapshots are already timestamped and sequential, which is exactly
the shape git wants; git diff between two snapshots is a more direct version
of the "save file comparison infrastructure" idea above.
Caveats: extracted saves run 60-160MB of text each, and CK3's save layout
(shuffled IDs, reordered blocks between snapshots) may not delta-compress or
diff as cleanly as source code does — a playtest's worth of snapshots could
balloon a nested repo into multi-GB territory with noisy diffs. Worth a small
experiment (one playtest, a few real snapshots, an actual git diff) before
committing to it as a convention.
Status: parked — interesting, but we're deliberately not chasing it mid-playtest.
Revisit when we want a "historical record" tool and have a slow afternoon.
